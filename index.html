Warm-up · Who Am I? Nature Style

A team guessing game to start with energy, curiosity and surprising facts. No worksheets, no grammar quiz disguised as a game.

Play the game

Nature Detectives

Open the game link supplied for the class. Project it so everyone can see. Divide the class into teams and let them discuss each clue before giving an answer.

Open WHO AM I? – Nature Style ↗
  1. Divide the class into teams and choose the first team to play.
  2. Reveal one clue at a time. Teams can guess after any clue.
  3. Teams must say: “We think it is… because…”
  4. Keep score according to the game. Encourage teams to explain their reasoning.
  5. After the answer, ask: “What surprised you about this creature or plant?”

Vocabulary · Taking action

These expressions describe actions governments, companies and communities can take to protect the environment.

Understand the action words

create laws

make official rules

The government should create laws to protect forests.
don't allow

prohibit something

Some countries don't allow certain fishing methods.
pay a fine

pay money as punishment for breaking a rule

Companies that pollute rivers should pay a fine.
provide

give or make something available

The city should provide free recycling services.
reduce

make something smaller or less

We need to reduce plastic waste.
require

make something necessary or obligatory

The law should require companies to use less packaging.

Grammar from zero · Hope and wish

We use hope and wish to talk about things we want. But they do not mean exactly the same thing.

Step 1 · Start with the meaning

Hope = something we want and think is possible

When we use hope, we want something to happen or be true. We believe it is possible, or we expect it.

I hope it doesn't rain tomorrow.
I want good weather. I think it is possible.
I hope our city has a recycling program.
I want this to be true now. Maybe it is true.
I hope the government will create stronger laws.
I want this to happen in the future.
Step 2 · Wish is different

Wish = we want reality to be different

We use wish when we imagine a different situation. Often, the real situation is not what we want.

I wish fossil fuels didn't exist.
But they do exist. I want a different reality.
I wish my city had more parks.
My city doesn't have enough parks.
I wish companies would stop polluting rivers.
I want companies to change their behaviour.

Step 3 · Four useful patterns

MeaningStructureExample
Hope about the presenthope + present simple / present continuous / canI hope people care about nature.
Hope about the futurehope + will / can / present simpleI hope people will recycle more.
Wish about the presentwish + past simpleI wish people cared more.
Wish for changewish + would/could + base verbI wish companies would use less plastic.

Pattern A · Hope + present

Use it for something you want to be true now.

I hope + subject + present simple / present continuous / can
I hope our school recycles paper.
I hope the river is getting cleaner.
I hope people can understand the problem.

Pattern B · Hope + future

Use it for something you want to happen in the future.

I hope + subject + will / can / present simple
I hope the city will plant more trees.
I hope we can organize a clean-up.
I hope the new law protects wildlife.

Pattern C · Wish + past simple

The verb looks past, but the meaning is about the present. It describes a situation that is not true or not satisfactory now.

I wish + subject + past simple
I wish there were fewer cars.
I wish people didn't waste water.
I wish my town had more green spaces.

Pattern D · Wish + would/could

Use it when you want a person, organization or situation to change, or when you want something to become possible.

I wish + subject + would/could + base verb
I wish the government would act faster.
I wish companies would reduce packaging.
I wish we could protect every species.
Important: the past form does not mean past time here.
I wish my city had more trees. = My city doesn't have enough trees now.
Also: don't use would simply because you are talking about the future. Hope is normally used for a future expectation: I hope it will improve. Use wish + would to express a desired change: I wish the company would change its policy.

Step 4 · Compare the meaning

SentenceWhat does the speaker mean?
I hope the city builds more bike lanes.I want this to happen and think it is possible.
I wish the city built more bike lanes.It doesn't have enough bike lanes now.
I wish the city would build more bike lanes.I want the city to take action.

Speaking · Hopes for a better planet

Use the new grammar to talk about real environmental priorities and negotiate solutions.

Speaking 1 · Think and prepare

Environmental priorities in our country

  1. Which environmental issues are most important in your country? Why?
  2. What do you wish your country would do about these issues?
  3. What do you hope your generation will do about environmental problems in the future?
Model:
A: I think air pollution is a serious problem in big cities.
B: I agree. I wish the government would improve public transport. I also wish there were more bike lanes.
C: I hope young people will support cleaner ways to travel.
Speaking 2 · Team mission

THE FUTURE WE WISH FOR 🌱

Your challenge: Create a short campaign pitch for a better environmental future. Your group must choose one local problem, imagine the change you want, and persuade the class that your plan is worth supporting.

Community reporter

Describe the problem and explain who is affected.

Future thinker

Describe what you wish were different today.

Action planner

Propose what you wish the government, companies or citizens would do.

Campaign speaker

Deliver the group's final 45–60 second pitch.

  1. Choose a problem: plastic waste, polluted rivers, air pollution, deforestation, endangered species or water waste.
  2. Describe today's reality: What is happening? Who or what is affected?
  3. Imagine a better present: Write two sentences with I wish + past simple.
  4. Demand a change: Write two sentences with I wish + would/could.
  5. Express an achievable hope: Write one sentence with I hope....
  6. Pitch: Present your campaign. The class votes for the clearest, most convincing and most realistic idea.
Language target: use at least one present hope, one future hope, two present wishes and two wishes for change.

Confident Communicator · Keep talking

Use these phrases to make your discussion more natural and productive.

Introducing problems
  • One problem we need to address is...
  • The biggest issue in our area is...
  • Another serious concern is...
  • This affects people because...
  • We shouldn't ignore...
Giving reasons
  • In my opinion...
  • The main reason is that...
  • What worries me most is...
  • This matters because...
  • From my point of view...
Agreeing and responding
  • I completely agree.
  • That's a good point.
  • I see what you mean.
  • I'd like to add that...
  • That's true, especially because...
Disagreeing politely
  • I see your point, but...
  • I'm not sure I agree.
  • That may be true; however...
  • Have you considered...?
  • I understand, although...
Suggesting action
  • We should consider...
  • One practical solution would be...
  • The government needs to...
  • Companies should be required to...
  • What if we...?
  • We could start by...
Expressing hopes and wishes
  • I hope our generation will...
  • I hope the city can...
  • I wish people cared more about...
  • I wish companies would...
  • I'd like to see a future where...
Reaching agreement
  • Could we combine these ideas?
  • What should we prioritize?
  • That sounds reasonable.
  • Can we agree on...?
  • Let's go with this plan because...
Presenting your campaign
  • Our campaign is called...
  • We believe that...
  • We hope to...
  • We wish our community would...
  • Our first step will be...
